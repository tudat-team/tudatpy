# Fixed-ephemeris LLR reference cases and array signature

Research checkpoint: 15 October 2026. No residual calculation, estimation or comparison-software execution has been performed. An optical native implementation and an unexecuted diagnostic script have now been prepared separately; their validation remains pending.

## What can be promised

A fixed published ephemeris supports a useful prefit observation-model comparison. It does **not** establish that observed-minus-computed residuals will equal APOLLO's intrinsic measurement noise. Replaying an already estimated published parameter set is compatible with “no estimation”; silently fitting a station offset, reflector coordinates, a nightly constant or a trend is not.

[Park et al. (2021), DE440/441](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/Park.2021.AJ.DE440.pdf), Table 3 and section 5.1: DE440 used APOLLO measurements from 2006–2017; recent LLR residual RMS is about **1.3 cm one-way**. DE440 is recommended over DE441 for modern observations. These are residuals of the ephemeris solution, not a guarantee for a separate implementation using arbitrary station parameters. Table 1 gives DE440 PA reflector positions; for Apollo 15 the position is **(1554678.104, 98094.498, 765005.863) m**. Those coordinates must accompany DE440 PA orientation, not another ephemeris's PA frame. Report residual distance as `c*(observed_RTT-computed_RTT)/2`.

[Battat et al. (2023), APOLLO dataset characterization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.11128), sections 3.1 and 3.3: the authors explicitly distinguish ephemeris inadequacy from measurement uncertainty. Median individual normal-point uncertainties are **3.4 mm raw / 4.2 mm including suggested systematics**; median nightly uncertainty is **1.7 mm**, and **1.0 mm** since September 2016. These are not fixed-DE440 residuals. No source found here promises zero mean and millimetre RMS for a new fixed-ephemeris reduction.

Numerical target proposal: keep the measurement uncertainties unchanged; aim initially to explain a **centimetre-scale** absolute residual, then examine bias and structure. The literature numbers are context, not a new pass/fail tolerance. A noise-limited claim requires demonstrably matched observation and nuisance parameters and residuals statistically consistent with the supplied uncertainties. Never subtract the measured mean to make a prefit comparison pass. Report count, weighted/unweighted mean, RMS about zero, scatter about mean and normalized residuals separately, with a fixed selection and no residual-based tuning.

## Candidate A: calibrated APOLLO points with DE440

The [APOLLO public normal-point archive](https://tmurphy.physics.ucsd.edu/apollo/norm_pts.html) provides Group e, **915 points from 2016-09-12 to 2019-08-18**, replaced on 2022-01-05. Its notes identify the start of the Absolute Calibration System (ACS). The old JPL format specifies UTC **launch** epoch, round-trip interval in `1e-13 s`, uncertainty in `0.1 ps`, reflector number, station identifier, meteorology and wavelength. It is fixed-width: whitespace splitting corrupts empty/narrow fields. This release is public without a collaboration requirement; no explicit redistribution license is stated on that page. The current ILRS CRD archive may be updated more frequently, so do not substitute an unpinned newer release.

A compact first selection is **all 11 records dated 2017-03-09**, preserved verbatim in `../data/llr/apollo_20170309_np2.txt`. It lies inside DE440's stated APOLLO fit span and after the ACS start, without selecting on residuals. First record, from source line 134 (one-based):

```text
512017 3 9 11915000000024761416294699370610466   137306B 73073  7525 5320A 251A
```

Documented decoding: UTC **2017-03-09 01:19:15.0000000**, RTT **2.4761416294699 s**, Apollo 15 (code 3), station 70610, 466 photons, uncertainty **13.7 ps**, pressure **730.73 mbar**, temperature **7.5 Celsius**, relative humidity **25%**, wavelength **532.0 nm**, duration **251 s**. About 2.05 mm one-way follows from the stated timing uncertainty. Treat this as a parser/observation input, not an independently published expected residual.

Required matched orientation products are [`moon_pa_de440_200625.bpc`](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/pck/moon_pa_de440_200625.bpc) and [`moon_de440_250416.tf`](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/fk/satellites/moon_de440_250416.tf), with [`de440.bsp`](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/spk/planets/de440.bsp). Use explicit **MOON_PA_DE440**, not a mutable generic alias or IAU_MOON. The NAIF frame file warns that even the two nominally similar DE421/DE440 mean-Earth realizations differ by up to about 53 cm over 2000–2040. SPICE should provide geometric body states; the observation solver supplies the two light-time legs rather than applying a second apparent-state correction.

**Remaining obstacle to an exact DE440 residual reproduction:** the inspected public `header.440` supplies ephemeris/dynamics constants, not the full station-coordinate/velocity, displacement, instrument-bias and observation-reduction parameter bundle. The [APOLLO telescope survey note](https://tmurphy.physics.ucsd.edu/apollo/doc/APO_coords.pdf), section 2, estimates its survey accuracy at **0.1 m**; the many printed digits on the normal-point web page do not make it a millimetre station solution. Using it is suitable for an explicitly approximate first reduction, not a noise-level acceptance criterion. The 2022 recalibrated NP release also need not equal the observation version used to estimate DE440. Thus the ephemeris, reflector coordinates, orientation and observations are available, but a complete exact published end-to-end DE440 parameter set has **not** been established here.

## Candidate B: INPOP21a as a more explicit parameter reference

The [official INPOP21a report](https://www.imcce.fr/content/medias/recherche/equipes/asd/inpop/inpop21a.pdf), section 2.4, uses **KEOF COMB2019** Earth orientation. Table 4 reports one-way weighted RMS, including **1.06 cm APOLLO 2013–2016**, **1.01 cm Grasse 2017–2020**, and **0.79 cm Wettzell 2018–2020**. Tables 5–8 publish lunar tidal parameters, libration corrections, station/reflector coordinates and period-dependent station biases. Table 8 biases are explicitly **two-way light-time distance in cm**; do not apply them as one-way metres. For example, the APOLLO 2010-11-01 to 2012-04-07 entry is −6.28 cm in that convention.

This is a promising replay of published estimated parameters with no new fit, but not a ready exact fixture: Table 7 does not state its station reference epoch/complete frame-and-tide convention alongside the numbers. These must be verified before propagation. Its PA reflector coordinates differ from DE440's and must never be mixed. Availability of tables alone does not prove all calibration, editing, clock, surface-tide and libration conventions match a new reduction. Keep this as a second case rather than borrowing INPOP's station coordinates to claim a DE440 reproduction.

## Essential observation conventions

The following is the proposed implementation contract, inferred from the physical observable; it is not a claim that a spatial station correction alone implements a complete relativistic LLR model.

1. **Three events and two legs.** Fix the measured UTC launch epoch, convert it precisely, and solve for reflection and reception epochs. Evaluate the moving transmitter, reflector and receiver at their own events. The [IERS TN34 LLR chapter, section 3](https://www.iers.org/SharedDocs/Publikationen/EN/IERS/Publications/tn/TechnNote34/tn34_097.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2) describes the recorded atomic-time interval and UTC launch tag, two-leg propagation, gravitational delay and endpoint time transformations. Do not compare a coordinate-TDB interval directly with calibrated atomic RTT. Account for topocentric time transfer and the station clock's actual reference convention; if working with raw proper time, include the proper-to-coordinate conversion exactly once.
2. **Matched spatial frames and scaling.** Use consistent barycentric axes, TDB-compatible ephemeris distances/GM, terrestrial TT-compatible coordinates and the declared conversion between them. A same-event spatial approximation is not a complete station worldline at barycentric time. In particular, omitted simultaneity/acceleration terms cannot support an unqualified millimetre claim. Preserve split/Time precision in endpoint subtraction and both light-time legs.
3. **Both surfaces move.** Earth orientation must have the selected UT1/polar motion/CIP data, rates and provenance. Stations need the published reference epoch, velocity, physical axis-intersection point, tides, loading, pole effects and relevant displacement history. Lunar PA coordinates need the same ephemeris orientation plus reflector surface tides and their solution-compatible Love numbers/permanent-tide convention. Do not reintegrate already published lunar rotation or add dynamical libration corrections twice.
4. **Propagation.** Include the required gravitational light-time delay for both legs in one consistent formulation. Solar and local-body effects are not supplied by merely rotating a station. Optical neutral-atmosphere **group** delay uses actual wavelength and measured pressure/temperature/humidity, with separate launch/receive geometry. [Mendes & Pavlis (2004)](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2004GL020308) supplies an optical zenith-delay definition; combine it with its selected mapping convention, not a radio default atmosphere. Its submillimetre zenith validation is not a guarantee for unknown gradients at low elevation. Check the NP/CRD flags before correcting something already corrected.
5. **Reduction and instrumental reference.** Keep ACS/version, axis-delay, detector/pixel, calibration and photon-selection conventions attached to the data. The [APOLLO calibration paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.11174), section 2, forms normal points by fitting the instrumental fiducial profile convolved with the orientation-dependent array profile and restoring the time trend. Therefore an existing normal point is not an unprocessed single-photon timestamp; do not add a second arbitrary “array correction” to its fitted centre. Its measured uncertainty and empirical systematic terms need distinct treatment.

## Minimal observer-direction array model

This is an independent first-order geometric proposal, not a copied software implementation. Let `p_i` be the fixed position of cube i relative to the **declared fitted array reference point**, expressed in fixed array axes. Let `R` rotate those axes into the lunar PA frame, and `u_tx`, `u_rx` point from the reflector toward the retarded transmitting and receiving stations, expressed in that same PA frame at the bounce event. Then

```text
round_trip_delay_i = -(u_tx + u_rx) dot (R p_i) / c
one_way_range_i   = c * round_trip_delay_i / 2
```

Sign check: moving a cube toward the observer shortens the range. For a static monostatic limit this is `-u dot (R p_i)` in one-way metres. In an exactly symmetric array with equal return weights the mean is zero even though its pulse is broad. Report weighted mean and variance separately. A filled rectangular panel gives the convolution of two uniform projected coordinates (a trapezoid, or a triangular limit); a known cube layout gives a discrete distribution. Convolve the return distribution with the laser/detector response only when modelling photons or the NP estimator. Do not turn width into a deterministic range bias.

Minimal inputs: declared reference point, fixed cube/panel geometry, array-to-PA mounting orientation, the two local observer directions and explicit return weights. Equal weights are a documented geometric approximation. Thermal deformation, cube optical depth, polarization/diffraction, illumination, velocity aberration, detector gating and first-photon selection can affect weights and centroids; geometry alone cannot infer them. Do not force the panel to face the observer at every epoch: its fixed mounting plus lunar libration is the physical source of the signature. Finite-photon variance can use the effective photon count only if the published uncertainty has not already included that contribution.

Use analytic controls (centred equal-weight array has zero mean; sign and width under reversed viewing tilt; zero tilt; weighted off-centre cube) and a published panel layout later. Do not add an estimator, general optical framework or free nightly bias merely to make prefit residuals look small.

## Source reuse and remaining decisions

Orekit's [MendesPavlisModel API](https://www.orekit.org/site-orekit-13.1.2/apidocs/org/orekit/models/earth/troposphere/MendesPavlisModel.html) identifies an existing optical-delay implementation for static inspection; GROOPS's [SLR station source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/groops-devs/groops/main/source/slr/slrStation.cpp) provides station-side organization to inspect. Neither establishes the complete LLR model or validates our conventions. No Orekit/GROOPS execution or comparison was made.

Next required provenance work is to establish the published station solution and observation version, complete time/clock transfer, lunar surface tide definition and range-bias sign. Until then Candidate A is a pinned input case with matched DE440 reflector/orientation products and an honest incomplete station model, not a certified expected O−C fixture. A modern-data requirement specifically favors DE440 rather than treating DE441's longer validity as improved accuracy.


## JPL 2020 reduction conventions now located

[Williams and Boggs, JPL LLR Model 2020](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/ftp/eph/planets/ioms/LLR_Model_2020_DR.pdf) is the exact reduction-model memo linked by DE440. Local copies are `JPL-LLR-Model-2020.pdf/.txt`. Section 3.11 evaluates lunar rotation directly in TDB. Section 3.12 gives lunar elastic surface Love numbers h2=.0423,l2=.0107,h3=.0234,l3=.0030; appendices give dissipative and constant tides. Sections 3.14 Eq16–17 require lunar LM=1.4825e-8, solar potential and the half-coefficient velocity contraction. This is not Earth's LC and not the newer TL-coordinate scaling. Section 3.18 says fitted reflector coordinates are array front-face centres, with per-leg internal optical delay nh/c (n=1.4555; Apollo h=.0275m, Lunokhod .0435m). This glass path is distinct from APOLLO's already-fitted normal-point profile centre. No geometric width correction should be added again.

The [JPL relativistic analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16147v4), Eq36, estimates Earth's omitted acceleration terms at about 1.34 micrometres. The rotation simultaneity term is larger: use the existing custom position callback for -((v_E dot r)/c²) times inertial rotational station velocity, before the existing terminal IERS11.19 spatial map. The script deliberately omits the negligible deformation velocity over microseconds. It does not apply a lunar retiming to DE440 PA orientation.

Matched kernels have been copied from the existing local NAIF cache into `../data/llr/kernels`, with SHA-256 and provider URLs in `SOURCES.json`. The frozen 200625 frame file maps explicit MOON_PA_DE440; the newer generic alias release is unnecessary when that explicit frame is used. Official FCUL routines are retained in this research directory and their original notice is included in the implementation repository. No kernel evaluation has yet been executed in this worker.


## Matera alternative with an independently published terrestrial frame

The [EDC Matera/Apollo15 record](https://edc.dgfi.tum.de/en/data/npt/2748806/) gives an actual532nm LLR point at UTC2022-08-21 03:48:26.60400001773 (seconds-of-day13706.60400001773). The complete [daily file](https://edc.dgfi.tum.de/pub/slr/data/npt_crd/apollo15/2022/apollo15_20220821.npt), now pinned locally, has **two** Matera7941 points (seconds-of-day10005.004000017177 and13706.60400001773); retain both if this becomes a case. CRD epoch_event2 is ground transmit at the system reference point, per [official CRD specification](https://ilrs.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/2022/crd_v2.01e3.pdf). Both sessions provide pressure/temperature/humidity, system-delay calibration metadata, and532nm wavelength. This is outside DE440's fitting span, but inside ephemeris validity; it is an independent prediction case, not a re-fit reproduction.

The [official SLRF2020 version250205](https://ilrs.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/2025/SLRF2020_POS+VEL_2025.02.05.snx) is also pinned. SITE/ID binds7941 to DOMES 12734S008. SOLUTION/EPOCHS has open-ended solution1 beginning2001; SOLUTION/ESTIMATE gives reference2015-01-01 XYZ(4641978.52389715,1393067.81970112,4133249.69590866)m, velocities(-.0186283100020115,.0190569144613420,.0146220896897415)m/yr. These printed values need the existing native SINEX/frame/tide/offset machinery, not a handwritten propagated coordinate. The file expressly states ITRF2020 and that applicable PSD corrections must be added. Matching the optical system reference point to this monument still requires the official station eccentricity metadata and validity intervals; the separate 2009 survey report is **not** being used or redistributed.

Do not confuse the CRD318ps/363ps bin RMS with a published normal-point standard error. The official format defines it as the spread of accepted individual ranges about the trend. They correspond to roughly48/54mm single-return one-way spread. Dividing by sqrt(13/74) would assume independent returns and yield roughly13/6mm, but that is **not** a supplied uncertainty or established noise target. Thus Matera solves the independently published terrestrial-frame problem more cleanly than the APOLLO survey, but does not automatically supply a certified cm residual target. No Matera model has been run and no selection based on residual has occurred.


## Station provenance follow-up during the first kernel build

**Matera optical reference point is established, rather than guessed.** The [official active MATM log](https://ilrs.gsfc.nasa.gov/network/stations/active/MATM_sitelog.html), revision 9 dated 2026-08-13, identifies CDP 7941/DOMES 12734S008 as the azimuth/elevation axis intersection. Its system 77/occupation 01 matches the pinned 2022 CRD headers. The [ILRS eccentricity definition](https://ilrs.gsfc.nasa.gov/network/site_procedures/eccentricity.html) states that permanent S-DOMES systems refer to the optical-axis intersection; the system eccentricity is zero. The current linked [XYZ eccentricity SINEX, version 260527](https://ilrs.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/2026/slrecc.260527.ILRS.xyz.snx), pinned locally, independently supplies7941/A/solution1, SOD 79417701, start 2000-01-01, open end, XYZ=(0,0,0)m. The official [annotated 200420 predecessor](https://ilrs.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/2020/RevisedEccentricityFiles-Release200420_ANNOTATED.pdf) gives the same open-ended zero offset in UNE. Together these support using the existing native SLRF2020 station history for this optical SRP without adding a local-marker tie. They do not remove displacement/clock/calibration obligations.

**A matched DE440 APOLLO fitted station vector has not been established.** Inspected primary products are Park 2021's article and its Tables 1–5, the public DE440 ASCII release/header, the JPL planetary-IOM directory, JPL 2020's reduction memo, and APOLLO's normal-point/survey documentation. JPL 2020 §3.3 gives the station reference epoch as J2000 with linear site motion, but does not publish the required APOLLO vector/rates. The public DE440 header has dynamical constants, not the complete terrestrial reduction parameter bundle. This is a bounded search result, not a claim that no public source exists anywhere.

A useful warning is [Williams and Boggs 2021, Testing the Motion of Lunar Retroreflectors](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2021JE006920), §3: the nominal ephemeris is DE440, but lunar orbit/orientation initial conditions, terrestrial station coordinates/rates, physical corrections and biases are adjusted. Its 9 mm WRMS is therefore **not** evidence that unchanged public DE440 plus an approximate station must reach 9 mm. It lists fitted station quantities without publishing their numerical values. Do not substitute its newly fitted reflector coordinates/rates for Park's fixed DE440 Table 1.

**INPOP21a station epoch/frame/tide convention is still unverified.** Its official [release README](https://ftp.imcce.fr/pub/ephem/planets/inpop21a/README.txt) documents planetary ICRF axes and TDB/TCB products, not the terrestrial station convention. The [TDB header](https://ftp.imcce.fr/pub/ephem/planets/inpop21a/inpop21a_TDB_m100_p100.header) includes a planetary integration epoch; this is not evidence for the epoch of Table 7's terrestrial positions. The official report gives station coordinates/rates and KEOF COMB2019, but its Table 7 and surrounding discussion do not explicitly identify station reference epoch, ITRF realization or permanent-tide convention. Earlier INPOP17a's explicit J2000/ITRF2005 statement must not be automatically inherited by 21a. No mixed INPOP station/DE440 ephemeris configuration has been introduced.

JPL 2020 §3.17 explicitly omitted lunar and Jupiter Shapiro in the fitted reduction. Our diagnostic retains lunar Shapiro as a physical addition, with this mismatch now recorded in the portable manifest/docs. Its quoted sub-mm lunar size is small, but should not be hidden when discussing exact reduction agreement.
